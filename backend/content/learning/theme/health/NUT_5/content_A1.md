# Nutrition — Topic 5: Reading Food Labels

## What Is a Nutrition Label?

Food labels tell you what is inside your food. Every packaged food has a **nutrition label**. Learning to read it helps you make better choices.

## Serving Size

At the top of the label, you see the **serving size**. This tells you how much food counts as one serving. All the numbers below are for one serving, not the whole package.

If you eat two servings, you eat two times the calories and nutrients.

## Calories

**Calories** tell you how much energy is in one serving. A small package can have many servings, so the real number of calories you eat can be higher than you think.

## The Ingredient List

The **ingredient list** shows everything in the food. Ingredients appear in order, from the most to the least.

- The first ingredient is the biggest part of the food
- The last ingredient is the smallest part

If sugar is one of the first three ingredients, the food has a lot of sugar.

## Hidden Sugar

Sugar has many names on a label. Look for these words:

- Sugar
- Corn syrup
- Cane syrup
- Fructose
- Dextrose
- Honey

These are all forms of **added sugar**. A food can say "healthy" but still have hidden sugar.

## Hidden Sodium

**Sodium** is the scientific name for salt. Too much sodium is not good for your heart. Look for these words:

- Sodium
- Salt
- Monosodium glutamate (MSG)

## Understanding % Daily Value

The label shows a **% Daily Value** for many nutrients. This number tells you how much one serving gives you, out of what you need in a full day.

- 5% or less is low
- 20% or more is high

Use this rule for sugar, sodium, and fat.

## Practice: Label-Reading Checklist

Now it's your turn. Take one packaged food from your kitchen. Read the label and answer each question.

**Step 1:** Look at the **serving size**. How many servings are in the package?

**Step 2:** Check the **calories**. Multiply by the number of servings you will eat.

**Step 3:** Read the **ingredient list**. Is sugar in the first three ingredients?

**Step 4:** Look for hidden sugar names, like corn syrup, cane syrup, fructose, dextrose, or honey.

**Step 5:** Check the sodium **% Daily Value**. Is it 20% or more?

**Step 6:** Decide: is this food a good everyday choice, or only a sometimes choice?

Use this checklist every time you shop.

## Key Words

- **Nutrition label** — the information panel on packaged food
- **Serving size** — the amount that counts as one serving
- **Ingredient list** — all the parts of the food, listed from most to least
- **Added sugar** — sugar put into food during processing
- **Sodium** — the scientific name for salt
- **% Daily Value** — how much a nutrient contributes to a full day's needs
