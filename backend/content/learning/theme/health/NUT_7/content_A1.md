# Nutrition — Topic 7: Healthy Eating on a Budget

## Why Healthy Food Feels Expensive

Many people think healthy food always costs more money. This is not always true. Some healthy foods are cheap. The problem is often what you buy, not healthy eating itself.

Fresh berries and pre-cut vegetables can cost a lot. But beans, rice, oats, and frozen vegetables are healthy and cheap.

## Buy Bulk Staples

A **staple** is a basic food you use often, like rice, beans, oats, or flour. Buying staples in a big bag or box is usually cheaper than buying small packages.

Good **bulk staples** to keep at home:

- Rice, oats, or other whole grains
- Dried or canned beans and lentils
- Frozen vegetables and frozen fruit
- Eggs
- Peanut butter or other nut butter

Bulk staples last a long time. You can always have healthy food at home, even with a small budget.

## Choose Seasonal Produce

**Seasonal produce** is fruit and vegetables that grow naturally at this time of year, in your area. Seasonal produce is usually cheaper and tastes better than produce from far away.

Ask yourself: what fruit and vegetables are in season right now? Buy more of those, and less of the expensive, out-of-season ones.

Frozen fruit and vegetables are also a good, cheap choice all year. They are frozen at peak freshness, so they still have good nutrition.

## Batch Cooking Saves Money and Time

**Batch cooking** means you cook a big amount of food one time, then eat it over several days. This saves money because you buy and cook in bulk. It also saves time, because you don't cook every single day.

Simple batch-cooking steps:

1. Pick one or two basic recipes for the week.
2. Cook a big pot of rice, beans, or a soup.
3. Divide the food into containers.
4. Keep some containers in the fridge and some in the freezer.
5. Reheat one container each day.

## Practice: Low-Cost Healthy Grocery List + One Batch-Cook Recipe

### Your Low-Cost Healthy Grocery List

Use this list as a starting point for budget-friendly shopping:

- Rice or oats
- Dried or canned beans/lentils
- Eggs
- Frozen mixed vegetables
- Seasonal fruit (whatever is cheapest this week)
- Onion, garlic, and carrots
- Canned tomatoes
- Peanut butter

### Recipe: Simple Bean and Rice Pot (Batch-Cook Style)

This recipe makes enough food for several meals.

**Ingredients:**

- 2 cups of rice
- 2 cans of beans (or 1 cup of dried beans, cooked)
- 1 onion, chopped
- 2 cloves of garlic, chopped
- 1 can of tomatoes
- 2 carrots, chopped
- Salt and pepper

**Steps:**

1. Cook the rice in a pot with water, following the package instructions.
2. In another pot, cook the onion and garlic for 2–3 minutes.
3. Add the carrots, tomatoes, and beans.
4. Add salt and pepper. Cook for 15–20 minutes, until the carrots are soft.
5. Mix the bean mixture with the rice, or keep them separate.
6. Divide into containers for the week.

This one recipe gives you several cheap, healthy meals, ready whenever you need them.

## Key Words

- **Staple** — a basic food you use often, like rice or beans
- **Bulk staples** — basic foods bought in a large amount, usually cheaper this way
- **Seasonal produce** — fruit and vegetables that grow naturally at this time of year
- **Batch cooking** — cooking a large amount of food one time, to eat over several days
